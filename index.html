Research Domain

Project Scope

Understanding dyscalculia, existing digital tools, and the gaps our platform addresses.

1

Dyscalculia and Its Impact

Developmental dyscalculia is a specific learning difficulty that affects number understanding, arithmetic and mathematical reasoning. Global estimates suggest it affects roughly 3–7% of school-age children, linked to weak number sense, working memory, attention and processing speed [1]–[3]. In Sri Lanka, limited awareness and few diagnostic tools mean many children are identified late.

2

Digital Tools for Dyscalculia

Programs such as Calcularis [12] and The Number Race [11], and apps like Let's Learn Math and EZMath, have shown benefits. However, most focus on remediation or one-time screening and provide limited continuous analytics for teachers and parents [4].

Research Gap

Identified limitations in existing digital support

Most systems judge learners only by correct/incorrect answers, ignoring response time, attempts and error patterns.

Cognitive abilities are rarely profiled from real-time interaction data.

Learner profiling and instruction are usually separate; assessment is one-off.

Many educational games are static and do not adapt difficulty, rewards or pacing.

Feedback is often generic, marking answers wrong without addressing misconceptions.

Components work in isolation; combined signal is never used for early warning.

Teachers and parents get scores but little interpretable insight into difficulty evolution.

No unified system joining cognitive profiling, mastery estimation, game adaptation and risk prediction.

The Problem

Same practice for different needs

Children with dyscalculia receive the same practice as their classmates even though their difficulties differ. Teachers have limited time to find the exact reason behind each mistake, and parents have little guidance. Existing tools are fragmented, adapt only on scores, and rarely warn adults before a child falls far behind.

Our Solution

One connected learning cycle

A research-backed platform of four connected components that form one continuous cycle:

Learner Interaction Data Collection Profiling & Estimation Adaptive Games Risk Prediction Intervention

Research Objectives

1

Profile Cognitive Skills

A supervised ML model analyses accuracy, response time and attempts to classify four cognitive dimensions.

2

Personalize Math Learning

BKT estimates mastery after every interaction, adjusts difficulty and provides context-aware hints — target 20%+ gain.

3

Adapt Game-Based Learning

Dynamic difficulty, content, pacing and rewards using a rule-based model benchmarked against RL.

4

Predict Learning Risk

Group learners into four difficulty profiles with K-Means and GMM; predict Low, Moderate or High risk with Logistic Regression.

Our Approach

Methodology

A systematic Design Science Research approach to building an adaptive learning platform.

1

Requirement Gathering

Requirement analysis and literature review of existing dyscalculia tools; identification of needs for Grade 2–3 students, teachers, parents and administrators.

Jan–Feb 2026
2

Data Collection

Learner responses, correctness, response time, attempts, error patterns and engagement collected at Sound Mind Academy, Koswaththa, with parental consent and anonymized IDs.

3

Data Preprocessing

Handled missing records, removed duplicates, normalized values and extracted features such as accuracy, response time, error patterns, progression and engagement.

4

Model Development

Decision Tree, Random Forest and Gradient Boosting for profiling; BKT for mastery; rule-based and RL for games; K-Means, GMM and Logistic Regression for analytics.

5

System Integration

Mobile learner app, FastAPI backend, shared learner database and APIs connecting the four components.

6

Testing & Validation

Macro F1, mastery progression, challenge-fit, engagement, Silhouette Score and risk metrics; pre/post-test comparison; teacher and student feedback.

Quality Assurance

Rigorous metrics, testing and ethical safeguards

Performance Metrics

  • Classification: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Macro F1
  • Learning: Mastery progression, error reduction (≥20%)
  • Games: Completion, challenge-fit, engagement, safety
  • Analytics: Silhouette, Davies-Bouldin, F2, AUC-ROC

User Testing & Validation

  • Teacher and therapist questionnaires (5-point Likert scale)
  • Simple smiley or star feedback from students
  • Observation of system usage data
  • Pre-test and post-test comparison

Ethics & Privacy

  • Parental informed consent
  • Voluntary participation
  • Anonymized learner identifiers
  • Secure storage and role-based access
  • Data used only for academic research
